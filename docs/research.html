I scanned 100 of the world's most popular websites. Here's how secure they really are.

Every site uses HTTPS now, but that's only the first layer. I ran SiteSentry against 100 widely used websites (99 responded) to see who also turns on the browser defences, modern TLS and email anti-spoofing that limit the damage when something goes wrong.

Key findings

What stood out after scanning 99 homepages.

  • Only 31% earn an A or better. The typical site scores 77/100 (grade B). HTTPS is universal, but the extra layers that limit damage when something goes wrong are not.
  • 52% have no Content-Security-Policy at all, and only 6% have a strong one. Of the 48 sites with a CSP, 42 weaken it with 'unsafe-inline', 'unsafe-eval' or wildcards.
  • 13% still accept TLS 1.0 or 1.1, protocol versions formally deprecated by RFC 8996 in 2021. Often this is kept for old devices, but it widens the attack surface.
  • Email is the best-defended area: 99% publish SPF and 91% enforce DMARC. Big brands are prime phishing targets, and it shows. The browser-side headers that protect visitors lag far behind.
  • 36% publish a security.txt and 23% set a Permissions-Policy: the cheapest wins on this list, at a few lines of config each.

Methodology

How the scan worked

  • Each site's homepage was scanned once on 2026-11-12 with SiteSentry, following redirects to the final page.
  • Only passive checks were used: normal HTTP requests, TLS handshakes and public DNS lookups. No port scanning, no attack payloads.
  • Certificates were validated against Mozilla's CA bundle, so results don't depend on one computer's trust store.
  • Fully reproducible: python research/scan_sites.py then python research/build_page.py.

Limitations

  • A homepage is one page: logins and APIs may be configured differently.
  • CDNs and bot protection can serve scanners different headers than browsers.
  • A missing header isn't a breach. These are defence-in-depth layers, and some sites have good reasons for their choices.
  • The site list is a hand-picked set of popular sites across many sectors, not a strict traffic ranking.
